When should you replace your roof?

Your roof is the most expensive thing you never think about — until it isn't. Replacing it early is far cheaper than repairing water damage after a heavy rainy season. Here are the signs we look for on every inspection.

  • Stains on ceilings. Brown patches after rain mean water is getting through. The longer they sit, the more rot and wiring damage they hide.
  • Cracked or missing tiles. A few loose tiles after a storm is normal; widespread cracking or moss growing between tiles is not.
  • Age. Most roofing materials in Lagos last 15–25 years. If yours is past 20 and leaking, replacement beats patching.
  • Daylight in the attic. If you can see light through the roof deck, the waterproofing layer is already gone.

If two or more of these apply to you, book a free inspection. Even when a full replacement isn't needed yet, knowing the state of your roof is worth the hour.

Kaya tip: Never patch a roof more than twice in the same area. Each patch hides what the next storm will find anyway.

How to budget a kitchen renovation

Kitchens are the room where budgets go to die — mostly because homeowners price the pretty part (cabinets, worktops) and forget the expensive invisible part (plumbing, electrics, waterproofing).

A realistic split for a typical Lagos kitchen looks like this: roughly 25% demolition and site protection, 30% plumbing, electrics and waterproofing, 30% cabinets and worktops, and 15% tiling, paint and finishing. If your quote's numbers look very different from that, ask why — a good contractor can explain the split line by line.

Three rules that save our clients money every time:

  • Don't move the sink and hob from their current plumbing positions unless you must — every metre of moved pipe costs twice what you think.
  • Buy appliances after the design is fixed, not before. A fridge that's 5cm too deep ruins the best kitchen plans.
  • Keep a 10% contingency in the budget even with a fixed quote — not for the contractor's surprise costs, but for the changes you'll decide you want halfway through.
Kaya tip: Get your quote before the festive-season rush. Contractors book out 6–8 weeks ahead from October, and prices on imported materials only go one way.

Tiling: DIY or professional?

We're a tiling company, so read this with the grain of salt it deserves — but the honest answer is: it depends on the room and the tile.

Do it yourself: a small floor (under 10 square metres) in a low-traffic room like a store room or balcony, with 60cm tiles or smaller. Straight-laid, no pattern. The cost of a bad job is low, and the learning is real.

Call a professional: anything over 10 square metres, walls, wet areas (showers and bathrooms), large-format tiles (80cm and up), or any diagonal or herringbone pattern. These need flat subfloors, careful adhesive coverage and experience — a lipped tile in a wet area is how you get cracked grout and standing water.

And the one thing everyone underestimates? Preparation. A professional spends a day levelling the floor before the first tile is placed. Skipping that is why DIY floors look great for a month and wobbly for the next ten years.

Kaya tip: Buy 10% extra tiles — the batch you buy next year to patch a broken tile won't match the one you have now.
